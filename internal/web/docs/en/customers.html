Customers and packages

Packages are mailbox plans

A package is the plan of one mailbox (Mail 10, Mail 100 …): the size of a new mailbox, the largest size it may have, and how much it may send per hour and per day. Mail is sold per mailbox, so a customer can hold several plans at the same time — for example three Mail 10, two Mail 100 and one Mail 25. Billing systems refer to a package by its short name (for example mail-10).

Every customer also has a base package. It holds what belongs to the whole customer: how many domains and aliases, how much storage all mailboxes may use together, how much the customer may send per hour and how many recipients one message may have. A new customer starts with as many mailboxes of the base package as that package includes.

  • Mail plans on the customer's page say how many mailboxes of each plan the customer has and how many are in use. A new mailbox can be made while a plan still has a free one, and it gets that plan's size and sending limits; staff can move a mailbox to another plan later. The REST API sets what was ordered with PUT /api/v1/customers/{id}/mailbox-packages.
  • Messages per hour and per day come from the mailbox's own plan, per hour for the whole customer from the base package. They protect the reputation of the server's address: a mailbox whose password was stolen cannot send thousands of spam messages. Messages over the limit wait in the queue.
  • Recipients per message stops one message from going to thousands of addresses.
  • A plan can be taken away only when no mailbox of it is left, and a package with mailboxes cannot be deleted.

Customers

A customer owns domains and mailboxes. Every customer's mail is stored under its own system user, so even a fault in the mail software cannot let one customer read another customer's mail. A customer can never route mail into another customer's mailboxes, send as their addresses or see their domains.

  • Panel logins: the customer signs in to the customer panel at the same address as the admin panel. Every login must set up two-factor sign-in at the first sign-in.
  • Billing ID: the customer's number in your billing system; the REST API finds customers by it.
  • Outbound address (when the server has extra outbound addresses, see Staff, settings and updates): the customer's mail leaves from its own IP address and host name, so its sending reputation is its own. Publish the SPF record shown on each of the customer's domains afterwards.

Suspend and terminate

  • Suspended: mail for the customer's domains is still received (nothing is lost), but nobody of the customer can sign in, use mail programs or send. Sessions end at once.
  • Terminated: mail for the customer's domains is refused. The data stays until it is deleted.
  • Activate brings a suspended or terminated customer back.
