Domains, mailboxes and aliases

Domains

On a shared server a customer's new domain receives mail only after it is verified: the domain page shows a TXT record (_emailzit.example.com) to put at the domain's DNS provider; then Check now. Staff can mark a domain verified by hand when they know it belongs to the customer; on a dedicated server domains need no verification (Settings → Mail server).

The domain page lists the DNS records the domain needs: MX (mail to this server), SPF, DMARC, and the records that let mail programs find their settings (autoconfig, autodiscover, SRV). Required records are marked.

Catch-all (mail for unknown addresses) is off by default: unknown addresses are refused during the SMTP dialogue, which keeps spam and backscatter away. Turn it on only when really needed.

Mailboxes

  • A mailbox has a size (quota) within the package; the usage is updated every ten minutes.
  • Its password is for webmail and the user's own pages only, always with two-factor sign-in.
  • Mail programs use app passwords that the mail user creates at /mail — one per program or device. A lost phone means revoking one app password, not changing everything.
  • A disabled mailbox keeps receiving mail but nobody can sign in. Staff can also block sending (for example after abuse) and set other sending limits for one mailbox.
  • Deleting a mailbox moves its mail to a trash folder on the server for 7 days, then it is gone.

Aliases and forwarders

  • An alias (e.g. info@) receives mail and delivers it to one or more addresses: mailboxes of the same customer or outside addresses.
  • A forwarder is an alias on the address of a mailbox. To keep a copy in the mailbox, list the mailbox itself among the destinations.
  • Send as: the mailboxes an alias delivers to may also send with the alias as sender.

Protection

  • Nobody can use the server to relay mail to other servers without signing in (no open relay).
  • Mail from outside that claims to come from a domain of this server is refused; signed-in users can send only from their own addresses and their send-as aliases.
  • Mail programs must use TLS; plain-text sign-in is refused.

Spam settings

Rspamd gives every incoming message a score; above 15 it is refused, and mail above the chosen level goes to the mailbox's Junk folder. The levels are Strict (score 4 and more), Normal (6, the default), Relaxed (10) and Never (spam stays in the Inbox, marked with an X-Spam header). Staff set the level of a customer on the customer page; it applies to all mailboxes of the customer that have none of their own. On the mailbox page (staff and the customer) and in the mail user's Auto-reply and filters page a mailbox gets its own level and lists of allowed senders (never Junk) and blocked senders (always Junk): addresses or whole domains, up to 200 each, compared with the From address. The rules run before the user's own filters and the auto-reply, so spam never gets an auto-reply. The mail helper writes them as a Sieve script into each mail home (.emailzit/spam.sieve) when they change and checks all of them every 10 minutes.

Moving mail from another server

On the mailbox page, Bring mail from another server copies every folder of the old mailbox over IMAP (port 993, or 143 with STARTTLS) with read flags and dates; Sent, Trash, Junk and Drafts land in the matching folders here. Messages that are already here are skipped, so run it once before and once after you move the MX record to pick up what came late. The old password is kept encrypted only until the import ends. On hosting servers imports only come from servers on the internet; a company server can allow private networks under Settings → Mail server.
